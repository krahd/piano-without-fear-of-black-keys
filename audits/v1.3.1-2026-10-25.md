# Editorial, musical and production audit — *Piano Without Fear of Black Keys*, v1.3.1

**Date:** 25 October 2026  
**Scope:** the complete published **53-page, 160 × 230 mm v1.3.1** PDF and its canonical v1.3 instructional source, retypeset without rewriting.  
**Source:** [current public PDF](../Piano_Without_Fear_of_Black_Keys.pdf); [manual Markdown](../piano_transposition_manual.md); [v1.3.1 stylesheet and build](../edition-design/v1.3.1/README.md).  
**Status:** critical review, **no edits to the published manuscript**. This audit deliberately **accepts** the stated purpose: a *transposition-first manual for piano, Ableton Push and Note, and guitar*, serving both a first-principles beginner route and a route for experienced musicians learning keyboard relationships. The separate successor book concerns a different pedagogical thesis and is out of scope.

## Executive judgement

**The premise is workable; its realisation is uneven.** The manuscript has a simple and reusable representation (degree/function → spelled pitches → register → instrument locations), a substantial set of correctly structured examples, original instrument diagrams, all-key reference tables and both short and longer practice programmes. The new typography is suitable in principle for a printed manual. The highest-return improvements are **repairing inconsistent pitch spelling, making the most important exercises actually executable, and correcting pagination and reference navigation**. A wholesale rewrite, new conceptual opening or expanded philosophy is not justified by this audit.

**Severity key:** P0 = factual error or exercise at risk of teaching a wrong rule; P1 = significant pedagogical or production defect; P2 = worthwhile polish; P3 = optional enhancement. "Verified" distinguishes direct source/PDF inspection or a programmatic check from an editorial judgement.

## Findings (with exact locations and suggested remedies)

### P0 — Degree formula contradicts notated final note in all twelve whole-tone rows (verified)

**Source:** Appendix B, *Whole tone*, approximately PDF pp. 43–44; the earlier *Common non-diatonic scales* table defines **1 2 3 ♯4 ♯5 ♭7**. Appendix B uses the same formula but spells the seventh-listed note as a **raised sixth**, e.g. **C D E F♯ G♯ A♯**. The pitch of A♯ is enharmonic to B♭, so this is **not a wrong piano key**; it *is* an inconsistent functional spelling relative to ♭7. For Db the final note is B where ♭7 is C♭, and this pattern repeats in all twelve roots.

**Evidence:** a programmatic degree/letter/pitch-class consistency check covered **204 scale/chord table rows** across Appendices A–C; the **only twelve flagged discrepancies** were the final degree of the twelve whole-tone rows. Other checked rows matched their specified formula and letter-degree spelling. This is a bounded table check, not a complete theoretical or editorial proof of the book.

**Remedy:** Retain the stated dominant-function formula **1 2 3 ♯4 ♯5 ♭7**, and respell that final note consistently as the correctly lettered **♭7** in each of the twelve whole-tone rows (e.g., C: B♭, Db: C♭). Alternatively, if intentionally showing the symmetrical all-sharp spelling, label it explicitly as an *enharmonic alternative* and do not present it as the same degree spelling. Add an automated degree–letter–pitch-class regression test.

### P1 — Key premise is not fully demonstrated in a start-to-finish playable example (editorial assessment)

**Source:** Preface (*How to read every musical example*), Part II §§4–7.1, Part V §16 (*translation pipeline*), exercises 8–10.

The proposed method requires deriving target notes, selecting a concrete register and choosing an instrument-specific realisation. The central progression example **Am–F–C–G → B♭m–G♭–D♭–A♭** gives roots and functions but stops **before explicit voicing/register/voice-leading choices**. The D-minor-seventh preface gives one sounding voicing, but the whole pipeline is never worked through for a short progression with actual pitches, octave numbers and alternative keyboard positions.

**Remedy:** Add **one** fully worked 2–4 chord example tracing source notes → degrees → target chord tones → chosen voicing/octaves → piano fingering/locations, followed by an equivalent exercise with fewer prompts. Do not add a new rhetorical chapter or repeat the theory in prose; the example should demonstrate the promised manual procedure.

### P1 — Push "Fixed" state is missing from key-change exercise (verified against manufacturer's manual)

**Source:** Part II §5, Part V §18 and exercise 9. Push's Scale menu has a **Fixed on/off** control. According to Ableton's current [Push 3 Reference Manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/push/manual/), **Fixed on** keeps the pitches anchored to pad positions as keys change, whereas **Fixed off** shifts the grid so its root follows the selected key. The book repeatedly instructs the learner to change the reference key while working with chromatic fourths but does not specify the Fixed setting or the expected resulting pitches.

**Consequence:** A student with Fixed on may change the displayed scale reference but fail to make the supposed transposition with the same gesture. This undermines the experiment, even though the book's general descriptions of Chromatic versus In Key modes are consistent with the manufacturer's documentation. Note's Fourths and In Key-off descriptions likewise agree with the current [Ableton Note Manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/note/manual/).

**Remedy:** Specify Push **Chromatic / Fourths / Fixed off** when the exercise intends a changed sounding root from a fixed pad shape, or deliberately choose **Fixed on** for a different experiment and explain it. Give one exact source-to-target pad/pitch test. Keep distinct the controller's visual scale highlight from actual note output.

### P1 — The 20-minute "all roots" routine is over-compressed for one advertised reading route (verified arithmetic + judgement)

**Source:** Part VI §20. The first five minutes require **one scale/mode in all twelve roots**, saying degrees and note names; the next five minutes require **one chord quality in all twelve roots, root position plus inversions**. Five minutes divided by twelve is **25 seconds per key** before naming, repositioning, checking accuracy or practising inversions. The beginner curriculum separately expects all twelve major keys by week three.

**Remedy:** Preserve the full twelve-root routine as an **advanced benchmark**; give a scalable beginner setting (e.g., three or four roots, accurate naming, full register check), clear intermediate progression and optional accuracy criteria. Avoid treating the eight-week programme as a universal attainment promise. Exercises should say whether they assess recognition, harmonic derivation, sound, fingering or tempo.

### P1 — Broken reference-book pagination and unbalanced production pages (verified from PDF render)

**Source:** PDF **p. 2 → 3** (example phrase “On D, that becomes:” is stranded just before the page break; **D F A C** begins p. 3); **pp. 4–5** (quick glossary splits, narrow cells break words such as *Transposition*); **pp. 52–53** (*One-page philosophy* begins on 52 and occupies a mostly empty 53); **p. 45** (five-column seventh-chord table set at very small type).

**Remedy:** Add explicit keep-with-next/keep-together rules for example introductions and their musical formulae, avoid a title beginning near page bottom, reorganise the quick glossary at book width, and start the final philosophy on a page where it actually fits; or remove its "one-page" label if necessary. For the chord appendix, consider separate tables of three rather than five chord families per row, or an appendix-specific wider page treatment. **Do not reduce text below the current small sizes** to fix layout. Re-render every page; no-overflow checks do not detect orphaned meaning or unreadable print scale.

### P1 — Musical description confuses preserved formula with literal chord identity (verified)

**Source:** Part II §4, comparison **Cmaj7 / D♭maj7**: “Both are 1 3 5 7. The musical object is identical; only its keyboard realization changes.” The **interval formula/chord quality** is identical, but the **notes and sounding chords are not**. That distinction is central to a transposition manual.

**Remedy:** Replace only the misleading sentence, e.g. “Both use 1 3 5 7. The chord quality survives transposition; the sounding pitches and keyboard shape change.” Keep the valuable example and surrounding prose.

### P1 — Reference navigation is incomplete, despite embedded PDF bookmarks (verified)

**Source:** PDF contents page (p. 6) lists material but provides **no page numbers**; the PDF has a machine-readable outline/bookmarks (122 entries), which partly mitigates this in a PDF viewer. The appendix tables are frequently visited and currently awkward to find from paper.

**Remedy:** Add numbered contents entries with appropriate page targets, a compact appendix contents table and functional clickable internal links in PDF. Keep the existing outline; do not remove it. Add running chapter/appendix heads selectively if tests show that helps more than it clutters the pages.

### P2 — Notation and terminology need a disciplined consistency pass

**Source:** Part II §5 (“in a In-Key layout”); throughout the text, there are choices among ASCII accidentals **b/#**, typographic flats/sharps, British/American spelling (**memorize/realize/color** in current text), scientific pitch notation, enharmonic double flats/sharps and the uses of “function”, “degree”, “pitch”, “pitch class” and “register”. These choices are not necessarily incorrect; **the manual needs one explicit convention**, particularly since it instructs novices in spelling.

**Remedy:** Apply the manual's own notation guide consistently; specify how to read double accidentals in the appendices and how they correspond to keyboard keys. Fix literal typos. Copy-edit in the chosen English variety without making the prose more generic or imposing a new rhetorical template.

### P2 — Reference information is comprehensive but not always usefully staged

**Source:** Appendices A–D; Part VII §25–29. The all-key tables are a useful asset for a transposition manual, yet same-sized 12-root tables in consecutive pages are taxing to navigate, and the theoretical spellings including Bbb, Abb, C##, etc. arrive without a sufficiently visible path back to the piano map.

**Remedy:** Keep the tables, but supply a concise legend for enharmonic spelling and a repeatable row-reading pattern; add cross-references to exercises. Test a reference-first page design with adequate type sizes, stronger continuation labels and grouped table families.

### P2 — Ear-based checks are named but not always operationalised

**Source:** Part III §9 (*Break the white-key trap*), Part VI weeks 2, 5 and 8, Appendix E (*Mode mutation*), Appendix F listening language. The manual asks readers to hear major/minor, Aeolian/Dorian or harmonic arrival but does not always give a stable tonic/drone or matched melodic passage against which the degree changes.

**Remedy:** Add a small number of tightly specified aural comparisons *within the existing transposition exercises* (fixed tonic, one changed degree, consistent rhythm, replay in another root). Optional original audio/QR examples would help, but the book should remain usable without an app, account or additional purchase. The expanded conceptual treatment of instrumental knowledge belongs to the separate successor book.

### P2 — Cover and reference publishing apparatus still need adjustment

**Source:** v1.3.1 cover, Appendix G, PDF metadata/link annotations. The title, author, version and original figures are present; however, the cover contains an unusually small reader-audience paragraph and motto. The PDF metadata's author field is blank. There are **only two active external link annotations** in the inspected PDF despite plain-text links in Appendix G. No print-specific bleed/ISBN package has been claimed.

**Remedy:** Include author in PDF metadata, convert source URLs into actual clickable links, consider moving the tiny cover explanatory text to the preface, and preserve the published trim/margins. If print-on-demand is planned, do a separate printer-specific preflight before asserting press readiness.

## What should not change

- The **transposition-first** organising promise: learn how note names, intervals, scale/chord formulae and instrument geometries support key changes.
- The beginner and experienced-player routes, unless the author later chooses otherwise.
- The technical/appendix character of the book; it is useful as a manual, not an essay.
- The restrained **160 × 230 mm** typography, 10 pt body and mirrored inner/outer margins as a starting point. The worst reference pages need **more layout care**, not global downsizing or redesign spectacle.
- Direct practical prose: no new 'learning outcome' boilerplate, motivational prefaces, repeated rhetorical slogans or resurfacing of the rejected [v2 prose](https://github.com/krahd/piano-without-fear-of-black-keys/pull/3).
- The **stable public filename** \`Piano_Without_Fear_of_Black_Keys.pdf\`; protect the existing laurenzo.net link and preserve old releases in the archive.

## Proposed work sequence (least disruptive first)

1. **Errata / notation:** fix whole-tone table letter/degree inconsistencies, the §4 transposition sentence, literal typos; add tests for interval-spelling charts, chord symbols, and sample voicings.
2. **Exercise feasibility:** clarify Push Fixed behaviour and exact steps; calibrate 20-minute and eight-week plans for both existing paths.
3. **One complete worked transposition:** end to end through chord spelling, fixed register and concrete piano voicing; pilot with one musician from each advertised reader path.
4. **Book production pass:** prevent broken musical examples, address p. 53, ensure readable appendices, improve contents/links and PDF metadata. Assess actual 100% printouts, not only rendered thumbnails.
5. **Pilot and second audit:** evaluate tasks for musical correctness, transfer and independent execution; log failure points rather than rewriting everything.

These changes are appropriate for a **maintenance or incremental edition of the existing manual**. They do not license replacing its argument with the separate geometry-and-musical-knowledge book.

## Verification scope and limits

- **Source inspected:** published v1.3.1 PDF (53 pages at 160 × 230 mm), original Markdown including preface, Parts I–VII, Appendices A–G, and the v1.3.1 CSS/build notes.
- **Automated check:** 204 rows spanning formula-based scales (Appendices A/B) and major/minor/diminished/augmented triads and seventh chords (Appendix C) checked for degree-relative semitone and note-letter consistency; 12 flags are the twelve whole-tone ♭7 spellings. This does **not** validate every prose claim, diagram, musical interpretation, transposition progression, or source citation.
- **PDF inspection:** individual text/page counts and fonts, link annotations and outline, all pages in four montages. Specific problems above are from observed page breaks; page-bound checks cannot establish reading quality.
- **Manufacturer-specific verification:** [Ableton Push 3 manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/push/manual/) and [Ableton Note manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/note/manual/), checked 25 October 2026.
- **Not done:** a full observational user test, physical print proof, line-by-line bilingual copy edit, historical musicology audit, or independent hardware verification on every Push/Note mode. These remain candidates for later work, not grounds for claiming the current edition is unusable.

**Publication state:** v1.3.1 remains current. This report is a plan for improvements, **not** evidence that any manuscript edits have been made.
