# Independent successor book — concept and research brief

**Date:** 25 October 2026  
**Status:** agreed as a **separate book project**, concepts preserved; **title, book-length scope, structure, repository, and final prose not yet approved**.  
**Parent publication:** *Piano Without Fear of Black Keys*, currently **v1.3.1**. This file is a lineage/decision record, not an addition to that publication.

## The two-book decision

1. **Existing book:** Keep *Piano Without Fear of Black Keys* as the practical **transposition-first** manual for piano, Ableton Push and Note, and guitar. Keep its two reading routes (absolute beginners and musicians already playing another instrument), its theory/reference use, and its existing publication identity. Improve it against **its own stated promise**; do not use the new project's ideas as a reason to repurpose or invalidate it.
2. **New book:** Develop an independent work for **musicians who already play, especially guitarists** whose learned shapes, chord vocabulary and improvisational habits may outpace their ability to name, explain, hear and reconstruct the notes and relationships involved. Its subject is **instrumental geometry and musical knowledge**, not a renamed transposition manual.
3. Establish the new book as a creative/editorial descendant (inspiration, selectively adapted examples, proper acknowledgement), **not** necessarily a literal GitHub fork or a new version of the old manuscript. Give it its own title, versioning and repository **only after the concept has passed a book-length test**. This present file is stored in the existing canonical repository so the agreement is not lost.
4. The separately discussed **absolute-beginner preparatory companion** remains a third, tentative concept; it is **not** the new musician-first successor and should not be silently conflated with it.

## Core problem

A musician can know a musical object through a physical routine without identifying the underlying notes. A guitarist may know the feel and sound of a D-minor chord, or a pentatonic position, while being less certain of chord tones, individual pitches and scale degrees. On piano, the fixed keyboard positions bring pitch identity into a different relation with fingering, while transposition changes white/black-key geometry.

These are pedagogical affordances and habits, not properties inherent to all guitarists or pianists. Neither instrument provides theoretical knowledge automatically; the same pitch may have several positions on guitar, and pianists also develop motor routines that can obscure pitch relationships.

The book should examine the difference between knowing **a sound, a gesture, a pitch set, an interval structure, a voicing, a tonal centre and a musical function**, using specific playable comparisons. It must not reduce all of them to one formula or equate keyboard fluency with theoretical understanding.

## Anchor example 1 — two minor-pentatonic collections

- A minor pentatonic: **A C D E G**; G minor pentatonic: **G B♭ C D F**.
- The **unordered pitch-class collections share three of five notes: G C D**. Their other members are **A/E** and **B♭/F**, respectively.
- An equivalent **fixed-register comparison**: **G3 A3 C4 D4 E4** → **G3 B♭3 C4 D4 F4**. Keep G3, C4 and D4; raise A3 by one semitone to B♭3 and E4 by one to F4. The overlap becomes visible, although the first collection is deliberately re-ordered from tonic-first A minor.
- The retained pitches take different scale-degree roles: **G: ♭7 → 1**; **C: ♭3 → 4**; **D: 4 → 5**.
- On guitar, an A-minor-pentatonic shape moved two frets left to G minor **literally shifts every corresponding sounding note down two semitones**. The fixed-register pitch-set transformation is **not the same operation as transposing an exact melody**. Separate these operations explicitly. Also, the large overlap is particular to this scale pair, not true of every transposition.
- The important teaching contrast: a movable hand shape preserves **gesture** while replacing sounding pitches; comparing pitch sets in a single keyboard register can foreground **shared pitches** while changing their functions. A change of pitch collection does not by itself establish the tonic by ear.

## Anchor example 2 — the familiar chord and its pitches

- Open D minor guitar fingering **xx0231** sounds **D3 A3 D4 F4** from the lowest sounding string upward. It is a particular **voicing** of chord tones **D F A** (degree structure **1 ♭3 5**), including a doubled D.
- An A-minor-shaped movable barre **x57765** produces D minor; slide to **x68876** for E♭ minor. The fretboard grip is movable; the pitches change.
- Root-position piano D minor **D F A** uses white keys; E♭ minor **E♭ G♭ B♭** uses black keys. Chord quality / interval formula is preserved, not the literal sounding chord or the physical shape.
- Introduce distinctions only where the worked example needs them: **chord identity**, chord tones, voicing, octave/register, scale degrees, fingering.

## Other substantive musical problems worth testing

- **Modes and tonal centre:** hear Aeolian's ♭6 versus Dorian's 6 with the *same tonic* and accompaniment, e.g. D natural minor versus D Dorian (B♭ versus B). Relating D Dorian to the same pitch collection as C major is a useful secondary observation, not a replacement for establishing D as tonic.
- **Inversions, voicings and voice leading:** the same chord identity can be realised in different registers, and related progressions can preserve common tones or move individual voices economically.
- **Implicit and explicit knowledge:** distinguish being able to play by ear, improvise by shape, recover note names, explain an interval and transfer a phrase between instruments. None should automatically be treated as a prerequisite for the others.
- **Instrumental topography and musical memory:** what becomes easy to repeat, transpose, vary, recognise or overlook on guitar, piano and chromatic pad interfaces; avoid sweeping claims about embodied cognition without supporting evidence.
- **Listening and improvisation:** every proposed major theoretical insight needs at least one concrete musical operation that can be heard, practised or independently reproduced, not merely an attractive diagram.

## Proposed book-length viability test (not an approved contents list)

Can the following form a cumulative argument rather than five loosely related essays?

1. Musical knowledge embodied in chord and scale shapes.
2. Pitch identity, octaves, register, chord tones and instrumental locations.
3. Relationships among pitch collections, shared tones, tonal centres and modal hearing.
4. Chord construction, voicing, inversion and economical voice movement.
5. Translation and creative variation across instruments — when the same musical idea survives, and when it does not.

For each part, identify a distinctive playable problem, a false assumption the exercise tests, an existing-literature comparison, and a genuinely learnable outcome. If too little material remains after removing repetition, publish a **shorter book or essay** instead. Do not assume book-length novelty.

## Prose, research and design gates

- **Keep the original manual's direct explanatory approach as a comparison point.** The AI-generated v2 chapter from rejected [PR #3](https://github.com/krahd/piano-without-fear-of-black-keys/pull/3) is a negative exemplar; do not paraphrase or adopt its scripted learning-outcome rhetoric.
- The scholarly, instructional and musical-pedagogical literature must be reviewed **before novelty claims**. Compare guitar/keyboard pedagogies, fretboard and keyboard theories, embodied music cognition, auditory training, scale/set transformations and cross-instrument transfer. No claim of an unoccupied research niche is established here.
- Validate every note, octave, fingering and scale-degree example independently and **play the exercise** before publishing it. Record audio comparisons if useful; do not replace listening with an explanation of an emotional effect.
- Use a sober, readable book design derived where helpful from the v1.3.1 system, but design is downstream of editorial acceptance; a polished PDF is not a proxy for approved prose.
- Choose **title, audience statement, and full chapter structure** after a concise argument/outline passes a rigorous adversarial audit.
- Do not alter the published v1.3.1 book or its stable laurenzo.net PDF link from this project.
