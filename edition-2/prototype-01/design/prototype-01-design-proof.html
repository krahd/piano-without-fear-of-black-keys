01 / RECOVERING A FAMILIAR CHORD

Start with something
you can already play.

Play an open D minor chord on guitar: xx0231. You may know this gesture so well that you no longer think about it. But which pitches actually sound?

OPEN D MINOR · GUITAR

The chord contains four sounding notes, including two Ds at different octaves.

CHORD TONES
D F A→1♭35

These three note names constitute a D minor triad. Its minor third, F, distinguishes it from D major, where the third is F♯.

Three related, different descriptions

Chord identityD minor
Chord tonesD – F – A
Actual guitar voicingD3 – A3 – D4 – F4

The first tells you what chord it is; the second names its constituent pitches without octaves; the third specifies which pitches this guitar fingering produces.

02 / ONE FORMULA, DIFFERENT INTERFACES

Move the chord.
Watch the shape change.

A movable A-minor-shaped barre chord at fret five sounds D minor. Move it up one fret and it sounds E♭ minor. On guitar the grip survives; on piano the same chord quality takes a very different shape.

GUITAR · MOVABLE BARRE / x57765
D minor: D3–A3–D4–F4–A4. One fret higher: E♭3–B♭3–E♭4–G♭4–B♭4.
PIANO / D MINOR
D – F – A three white keys
PIANO / E♭ MINOR
E♭ – G♭ – B♭ three black keys
What stayed the same? The interval formula: 1–♭3–5. What changed? Every pitch, its location on the keyboard, and the most convenient movement of the hand.

Hear the alteration, not just the scale

While D minor continues in the background, alternate A–B♭–A and A–B–A. Then play the paired two-bar cells. The tonic, the accompaniment and the surrounding notes stay unchanged.

AEOLIANA – C – B♭ – A  |  F – G – F – D
DORIANA – C – B – A   |  F – G – F – D

Try it without a metronome first. Listen to the quality of the sixth degree against the sustained minor harmony. The exercise does not depend on assigning emotional adjectives to the modes.

04 / WHY TONAL CENTRE MATTERS

Starting on D does not
make the music Dorian.

C major and D Dorian contain the same pitch classes: C, D, E, F, G, A and B. But a pitch collection does not determine which note a listener experiences as the tonal centre.

SAME PITCH COLLECTION
C major ↔ D Dorian

All seven pitches coincide. What changes is the tonic and the musical context supporting it.

CDEFGAB
SAME TONIC
D Aeolian ↔ D Dorian

D remains the centre. Only the sixth degree changes; this gives us a controlled aural comparison.

DEFGAB♭ / BC

Which comparison answers your question?

Where do the notes come from?Compare shared pitch collections.
What makes Dorian sound Dorian?Keep D as the tonic; hear its natural sixth.
Playing a C major scale beginning on D, over a C major accompaniment, does not by itself establish D Dorian. Harmony, emphasis, phrasing and the experience of a tonal centre matter.

06 / LEARNING CHECK

Can you recover
the relationship?

Try these tasks without referring to the previous pages. The point is not speed or perfect technique, but whether you can find your way from a musical gesture to the relationships it embodies.

  1. Identify. From open D minor on guitar, name its three chord tones and then its four actual sounding pitches.
  2. Change. Turn D minor into D major by moving only one chord tone. Which one moved, and by how many semitones?
  3. Listen. Play D Aeolian and D Dorian against the same Dm7 accompaniment. Identify the degree that changes and improvise a phrase that makes it perceptible.
  4. Transpose. Without consulting the table, play F♯ Aeolian and F♯ Dorian. Identify their different sixth degrees.
  5. Compare. What is preserved when a guitarist moves a barre chord up one fret? What is preserved when a pianist rebuilds the same chord formula a semitone higher?
CHECK YOUR ANSWERS AFTER PLAYING

1. D–F–A; D3–A3–D4–F4. 2. F becomes F♯, a semitone higher. 3. B♭/B: ♭6/6 against D. 4. D/D♯ against F♯. 5. The interval relationships and chord quality; the notes and register change by a semitone, while the instrument geometry presents the new pitches differently.
